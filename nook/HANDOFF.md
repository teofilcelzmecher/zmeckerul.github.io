# Budgie (formerly Nook): where things stand

Read this first when picking Budgie up again (a new chat, or after a long break).

## Versions

- **Name:** the app is **Budgie** and its communities are **nests** (still `n/…`). It was called Nook until 0.2.1. Only what people see was renamed: the code, CSS classes, storage keys, the folder `nook/`, the server's `nook` command, service, user and paths, and the live repository's name all still say nook. The starter nests `nookhq` and `asknook` became `budgiehq` and `askbudgie` (the server moves their posts once; old links redirect).
- **Live:** 1.0.0, the first public version, with the server at https://nook.tailfb59a9.ts.net (the laptop, through Tailscale Funnel), set in `nook/js/config.js`.
  1.0.0 dropped the early-access wording and added:
  - **Languages** (`js/i18n.js`, `js/lang/<code>.js`): English, Romanian, Spanish, French, German, Italian. The page's loader in `index.html` picks one (`localStorage` `nook-lang`, else the browser's languages) and loads its file before `i18n.js`, which translates whatever the app writes (innerHTML, insertAdjacentHTML, textContent, the attributes in `ATTRS`, `M3.rollText`, and a MutationObserver for the rest) by exact sentence, by pattern (`"Report u/{0}"`; a slot after `u/` or `n/`, in quotes or before `’s` is a name and is never translated), by count (`"3 members"`, each language's plural rule in its `count`), or as a list. Anything inside `KEEP` (`translate="no"`, `.md`, post titles, comments, bios…) is left alone: put `translate="no"` on any element that shows a name or title people chose. Settings → Language (or the welcome page's button) switches; the choice is in settings too, so it follows the account. **When the app's English changes**: `cd nook/tools/i18n && npm install && node extract.js`, translate the new ids from `strings.tsv` into a `tr/*.tsv` file (`id`, then ro es fr de it; `=` keeps the English; a row starting `+` gives the English itself, for sentences the app puts together), then `node build-lang.js`. Untranslated words simply show in English. The Terms and Privacy Policy stay in English.
  - **Videos and GIFs in posts** (`server/video.js`, FFmpeg, which `install.sh` installs): `POST /api/videos` takes the raw file (up to 100 MB), FFmpeg makes an H.264/AAC MP4 of at most 60 s and 1280 px without metadata, frames go through the picture check (post strictness), a poster frame becomes an image, and the post points at it (`posts.video`, `videos` table, `DATA/videos/<id>.mp4`, served with Range). Unposted uploads go after 6 h; deleting or removing the post deletes the file. GIF posts are up to 6 MB, or a GIF found with KLIPY's search (the composer's Find a GIF, when `nook gifs` is on): type `gif`, `posts.gif` keeps KLIPY's link as JSON, checked against `KLIPY_MEDIA_RE` like chats, and shown from KLIPY (`art()`/`thumb()` in `views.js`). No FFmpeg: `bootstrap.videos` is false and the app doesn't offer videos.
  - **Up to four pride flags** (`users.pride_flag`, comma-separated; `publicUser` sends `flags` and the first as `flag`), a **crop step** for profile pictures and banners (`ui.cropPicture`; GIFs skip it), and **removing just part of a profile** (`PROFILE_PARTS`: photo, banner, bio, pronouns) from the person's ⋮ menu or the Moderation page.
  - **Chat colours** for one-to-one chats (`chat_themes`, `PUT /api/chats/:name/theme`, a `theme` line in the chat), **calm mode** that hides streaks and counts (dots instead) and skips confetti, a quieter Home, and icons that Google Translate can't break (`translate="no"` on every `.m3-icon`).
  - **Usernames checked by the text model** (`textmodel.scoreName`: splits names into words, undoes l33t; sure → refused, doubtful → an automatic report).
  - **Early Bird** only for accounts made before the server first ran 1.0 (`v1-since` in `meta`).
  - **Calls**: the self-view drags to any corner on phones (remembered per device), the camera flip shows only on phones and screen sharing only on computers, and the relay has a monthly cap: devices report the bytes they receive through it (`POST /api/calls/:id/usage`, counted ×1.1 into `meta` `turn-used-YYYY-MM`), optionally checked against Cloudflare's own hourly count (`nook turn-usage`), and at `turn-limit-gb` (950 unless set) every call ends with reason `limit` and new ones get 503 until the 1st of next month (UTC).
  It has accounts, friends, messages, live updates, reports and a Moderation page. Profiles have a picture and a banner (each a photo or a GIF: the GIF moves on profiles and chats, a still copy shows in feeds and when motion is reduced) and a bio of up to 300 characters with line breaks and formatting. Admins can clear a reported profile from the Moderation page or with `nook clear-profile <name>`. Sign-up is open (no code); `nook signups invite` brings codes back.
  0.7.0 made profiles much more customisable, all with Material 3 parts: pronouns (free text up to 24 letters, quick picks, word-list checked), one of 20 pride flags (drawn as SVG in `ui.PRIDE_FLAGS`; no Progress flag, as its licence is non-commercial and Budgie has ads) shown after the name everywhere, up to 6 interests (`ui.INTERESTS`) as chips, a profile colour style (`ui.PROFILE_SCHEMES`: the profile page wears its owner's colour in that Material scheme, when the viewer has "Wear nests’ and profiles’ colours" on) and the shape that floats behind the profile (`data-shapes` on M3 stickers, a small addition to `m3/m3.js`). The server keeps them in `users` (`pronouns`, `pride_flag`, `interests`, `profile_scheme`, `sticker_shape`) and checks each against its own copy of the lists. `nook clear-profile` clears pronouns too.
  0.8.0 added badges Budgie gives, shown as tonal pills under the name on profiles (`ui.SPECIAL_BADGES`): Developer (from `nook dev NAME`; u/teofil gets it by itself, once, while it's an admin) with a "Dev" tag on its posts and comments, Tester (a developer's "Make tester" button on a profile calls `POST /api/badges`, or `nook tester NAME`; the person is told) and Early Bird, which isn't stored: every account made before the server first ran as 1.0 has it (the server writes `v1-since` in `meta` then). Given badges live in `users.badges`, comma-separated. Two-step sign-in (an authenticator app) is the next thing the owner asked for (they put it off until later).
  0.9.5 was the last early-access version. Nests have teams: the owner (`nooks.created_by`; Budgie's admins for the starter nests, which get a row of their own in `nooks` the first time anything about them changes) gives people who joined a role in `nest_team` (Admin, Moderator, Helper or a custom title), each a list of `pin`, `remove`, `ban`, `rules`, `team` (`NEST_ROLES`; only the owner grants `team`, and nobody grants what they don't have). The nest's Manage page (`#/n/<id>/manage`: Team, Rules, Bans, Reports) and the post and comment menus use them; `POST /api/posts/:id/pin` (up to 3), `POST /api/nooks/:id/remove`, `/bans`, `PATCH /api/nooks/:id` (rules, tagline, description). `nest_bans` stops posting and commenting in that nest only (`notBanned`). Role tags show by names in a nest. Chats take pictures and GIFs (`messages.media`, `group_messages.media`: JSON, `{type:'image', id}` kept in `images` and checked like profile pictures, or `{type:'klipy', url}` kept as KLIPY's link). GIF search is KLIPY's, from the browser (`js/pickers.js`), once the owner runs `nook gifs KEY`; KLIPY's terms say searches and media must come from the client, unproxied, unreordered, with "Search KLIPY" in the box. `js/pickers.js` also has the emoji picker (`js/emoji.js`, from Unicode's emoji-test.txt, loaded on first use; shown where there's a mouse) and the picture viewer. Calls (`js/calls.js`) now open three transceivers per connection in a fixed order (sound, camera, screen), so cameras and screens switch with `replaceTrack` and nothing is renegotiated; any call can turn a camera on; screens share on computers (`getDisplayMedia`). A video call shows a camera preview first (`prejoin`), and the ring screen has a camera on/off chip (remembered per device). In a group call, People rings members again (`POST /api/calls/:id/ring`). A page closing mid-call is "away" for 30 s (`/away`, `NOOK_AWAY_MS`) and `bootstrap.call` lets the reloaded page rejoin. Keys in a call: M, V, Shift+S, R, C, Ctrl/⌘+D and +E. Settings → Calls: ringtone (synthesised) and Call look (colours, background). Minimised, the call is a slim bar at the top that pushes the page down (`html.has-call-bar`). Blocking (`blocks`, `POST /api/blocks`, `DELETE /api/blocks/:name`, `bootstrap.blocked`): it ends the friendship, and neither can befriend, message or call the other; group calls don't ring someone who blocked the caller; replies from them don't reach the inbox; the app hides their posts and comments and folds their group messages away. The blocked person isn't told. The app offers blocking only when the server sends `blocked` (a server without it gets the old buttons).
  0.9.0 added group chats and calls. Groups (`chat_groups`, `group_members`, `group_messages`; up to 32 people) start from your friends; anyone in one can add their own friends, the owner renames it (name, colour, one of 12 shapes) and removes people, and when the owner leaves the longest member takes over (an empty group is deleted). Each member has a `since_id` (they only see what was said after they joined) and a `read_id` for unread counts. News about the group (made, added, removed, left, renamed, new owner) and calls are stored as group messages with a `kind` and a JSON body, drawn as lines between the bubbles; DMs got a `kind` too, for call lines. Group messages get the same checks as DMs and can be reported (`gmessage`), as can a group's name (`group`); a long press (or right click) on any message opens Copy / Report. Calls (`js/calls.js`) are WebRTC between the devices: a mesh where everyone connects to everyone (`CALL_MAX` 6), with the newcomer making the offers so they never cross. The server only rings people (35 s) and relays offers, answers, candidates and mute/camera state over a Server-Sent Events stream, `/api/live`, which the app reads with `fetch` so it can send its sign-in header; when that stream can't stay open, rings arrive with the regular `/api/updates` and a call polls `/api/signals` every 1.5 s. Signals live only in memory for two minutes. A device silent for 45 s leaves its call. Devices find each other with Cloudflare's free STUN server, or through Cloudflare's TURN relay once the owner runs `nook turn KEY TOKEN` (then the app forces `iceTransportPolicy: 'relay'`, so nobody sees another's IP). Settings → Calls ("Let friends call me") sets `users.calls`. The call screen is always dark, tinted from the caller's or group's colour, with the face in a morphing M3 shape that swells as they speak (WebAudio meters); sounds are synthesised, nothing is downloaded. Tested with three real browsers (Chromium with a fake camera and microphone, phone and desktop sizes) and in WebKit.
  0.6.0 lets people pick a font for their name (Edit profile → Name font), shown on their profile, posts, comments, chats and in people lists; Settings → Name fonts turns them off for the reader. The fonts are eight OFL fonts in `nook/fonts/`, cut down to the characters a username can have (a few KB each; licences on `licenses.html`), and `ui.NAME_FONTS` / the server's `NAME_FONTS` list them. To add one: subset it the same way (fontTools, U+0020–007E, woff2), add it to both lists and to the `@font-face` rules in `app.css`.
  Sign-up has an optional robot check, Cloudflare Turnstile (0.5.2; 0.5.1 briefly used Google reCAPTCHA, which the owner swapped for Turnstile because the domain is already on Cloudflare and its keys are simpler to get). It's off until the owner runs `nook captcha <site key> <secret key>` (keys from the Cloudflare dashboard's Turnstile page; steps in `server/README.md`; the command checks the secret with Cloudflare first). The keys live in the database's `meta` table (`turnstile-site`, `turnstile-secret`) and `/api/health` hands the site key to the app, so turning it on or off needs no deploy. `js/account.js` only loads Cloudflare's script when the sign-up form opens; the server checks the answer with Turnstile's siteverify after every other check (so a taken name doesn't use up the answer) and only accepts answers passed on the site's own hostnames.
  0.5.0 added automatic checks on the server (below): pictures for nudity, text with a small AI model, and more word-list rules. Doubtful things are posted and land on the Moderation page as automatic reports.
  Coming from 0.4.0 the laptop needs `sudo nook update`, then `sudo /opt/nook/server/install.sh` once (it raises the service's memory limit from 512 MB to 1.5 GB, which the text model needs, and from then on `nook update` re-runs it with `--update` by itself). The server then downloads the text model by itself (about 300 MB, once); `sudo nook text-check` shows whether it's ready. `sudo nook scan-images` and `sudo nook scan-text` check what was posted before.
- **Ads:** Google AdSense in-feed ads between posts (home, popular, nest feeds only), in `js/ads.js`, off until `ads.client`, `ads.slot` and `ads.layoutKey` are filled in `js/config.js`. The build then writes `ads.txt`. Non-personalised by default; consent comes from Google's own message (AdSense → Privacy & messaging). Auto ads must stay off in AdSense, so there are no banners.
- **Earlier:** 0.1.2 was local-only, behind a client-side code gate. It's gone, and its eight codes don't work on the server.

## Updating the live site

1. Change the code in `nook/` and `m3/` on the source branch. Test it: `node nook/tools/api-test.js` and `node nook/tools/moderation-test.js`. If you changed what the app says, update the languages (see 1.0.0 above).
2. Build into a clone of the live repository: `python3 nook/tools/build-site.py <source repo root> <nook-early-access-website clone>`. Commit and push both.
3. Server changes reach the laptop when the owner runs `sudo nook update` (it pulls, then re-runs `install.sh --update`, which rewrites the service and restarts it).

## How the code is organised

- `nook/index.html` runs two small scripts written into the page (their sha256 fingerprints are in its Content-Security-Policy, with `'strict-dynamic'`, so only they and what they load can run; `python3 nook/tools/csp.py nook/index.html` refreshes them, and the build does it too). The second loads (m3) → `config.js`, `api.js`, `account.js`. Someone not signed in sees the welcome page (`#landing`, plain HTML in `index.html`, so search engines and AdSense can read it; `account.js` wires its buttons and the nest colour demo), and `og.png` is the picture link previews show. `account.js` signs in, fetches `/api/bootstrap` into `window.NOOK_BOOT`, then loads `data, store, ui, moderation, fx, views, pickers, calls, app` (and `emoji.js` when the emoji picker first opens).
- `store.js` holds the server snapshot and polls `/api/updates?rev&note&msg&gmsg` every 15 s (every 4 s while a chat is open). Writes are optimistic and undone if the server refuses.
- `server/server.js` is plain Node ≥ 22.5 with `node:sqlite`; nothing is installed from npm. `db.js` holds the schema, with revision stamps for deltas. `cli.js` has the admin commands; `install.sh` sets up the systemd service (memory limit 1536 MB), backups and lid handling.
- `js/moderation.js` holds the word-list rules. The browser and the server both load the same file.
- The server's automatic checks run in worker threads, and their libraries ship ready to run in `server/vendor/` (the build copies it; licences on `licenses.html`):
  - `server/pictures.js`: two NSFWJS image models on TensorFlow.js (WebAssembly), for every picture, banner and GIF frame. Pictures are refused if it can't run (it fails closed). Stricter for profile pictures and banners than for posts.
  - `server/textmodel.js`: Horizon Labs' multilingual-toxicity-small (an mmBERT, 34 languages, Romanian included) on ONNX Runtime's native Linux x86-64 library. The model (268 MB) is too big for git, so the server downloads a pinned version from Hugging Face into `/var/lib/nook/text-model` and checks its sha256. The tokenizer is ours in JS and matches Hugging Face's exactly. It skips itself (and the word lists carry on alone) when it can't run: another platform, under 2.5 GB of RAM, or a service memory limit too low. It uses about 550 MB and 20–40 ms a message. Thresholds per kind of harm are in `LIMITS` (how they were chosen: see Testing); `nook text-check "some text"` shows what it makes of anything.
  - `NOOK_PICTURE_CHECK` and `NOOK_TEXT_CHECK` set to `off` turn them off (emergencies only); `test` scores by markers, for `api-test.js`.
- `promo/ad.html` is a 19-second vertical video ad cut to a 120 BPM beat, in the app's real dark look (open it in a browser to watch it, silently). `node nook/promo/render.js --music sunny` renders it to `nook/promo/budgie-ad.mp4` (1080×1920, 60 fps); `--music sunny,tropical,lofi` makes one video per style, and `--video old.mp4` reuses frames already rendered so only the music changes (seconds instead of minutes). `promo/music.js` writes the music note by note following the `SONG` plan in `ad.html`, plays it with FluidSynth and the MIT-licensed FluidR3 General MIDI instruments (`apt install fluidsynth fluid-soundfont-gm`, or `pacman -S fluidsynth soundfont-fluid`), and masters it to -14 LUFS with ffmpeg. `--stills 1,5,9` saves single frames, `--audio x.wav` just the music. Nothing in `promo/` goes to the live site.

## Testing

`node nook/tools/api-test.js` runs about 300 checks against a temporary server (the picture and text checks in test mode); `node nook/tools/moderation-test.js` checks the word-list rules on 225 English and Romanian sentences. The text model was tuned on about 150 English and Romanian sentences, many built to fool it, and checked on 60 it hadn't seen: with the word lists it stopped or flagged about half the harmful ones there, and flagged 1 in 30 harmless ones for a look (none blocked). It misses indirect bullying ("everyone in class thinks you're a freak"), so people's reports still matter. Browser tests were written with Playwright during development: sign-up, posting, comments, friends, chat, moderation, every button on every page, CSP violations and phone layouts. Re-create them as needed.
