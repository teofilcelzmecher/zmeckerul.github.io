Privacy Policy

Version 1.0.0 · Last updated 30 October 2026

The short version

  • Your account, posts, comments, messages and settings are kept on Budgie’s own server: a computer run by Budgie’s developer, not a big tech company.
  • We don’t ask for your email, real name, phone number or location, and Budgie’s server doesn’t log IP addresses.
  • Budgie shows ads from Google between posts in feeds, never in chats. They aren’t personalised, and Google uses cookies for them only as far as you allow (section 6).
  • When you create an account, Cloudflare Turnstile may check you’re not a robot, usually without you doing anything (section 4). It’s only loaded on the sign-up form.
  • Voice and video calls, and screens shared in them, are encrypted between the devices in the call and never recorded: Budgie’s server never gets the sound or pictures. When Budgie’s relay is on, calls pass through Cloudflare, so the people in a call don’t see each other’s IP address; on a direct call, they can. If the relay’s free allowance for the month runs out, calls pause until the next month (section 4).
  • Short videos you post are converted on Budgie’s own server, which drops hidden details such as where they were filmed, and checked automatically like pictures. Pictures you send in chats are kept on Budgie’s server with the message, and checked automatically. If you search for GIFs, in a chat or a post, your search goes to KLIPY, a GIF service, along with your IP address, and GIFs found that way are shown from KLIPY’s servers (section 4).
  • Budgie itself uses no cookies, analytics or tracking, and we never sell your information or give Google your posts, messages or account.
  • To keep Budgie safe, what you post and send, private messages and usernames included, is checked automatically on Budgie’s own server, by word lists and two small AI models. Nothing is sent to another company for this, and a moderator only sees a private message that’s reported or flagged (section 8).
  • You can download everything we hold about you, or delete your account, any time in Settings → Account.

  • Our legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f)): keeping Budgie secure and running (rate limits, backups, hosting, and the robot check that stops fake accounts) and keeping it safe (moderation, the automatic checks in section 8, and reports). You can object; contact us.

  • Legal obligations (Article 6(1)(c)): when the law requires us to act on illegal content or to answer the authorities.

4. Where it’s kept, and who else is involved

Budgie’s server is a single computer looked after by Budgie’s developer. The database on it is protected by the computer’s security, and nightly backups are kept on the same computer, with occasional copies on a storage drive in the developer’s care. As the person who runs the server, the developer can technically open the database, private messages included. We only do so to fix problems, handle reports about a specific message (including one the automatic checks flagged), or when the law requires it. The automatic checks (section 8) run on that same computer. The text model they use was downloaded once from Hugging Face, a website for sharing AI models; nothing about you or what you post is ever sent there.

Calls. To connect a call, your device and the others in it swap short technical messages through Budgie’s server: offers and answers saying which kinds of sound and video each can handle, and the network addresses each could be reached at, which include your IP address. The server passes them straight on and keeps them only in its memory, for two minutes at most. The call itself then goes directly between the devices, encrypted with WebRTC’s standard encryption, so Budgie’s server never receives its sound or pictures, or a screen someone shares. If your page closes or reloads during a call, the server keeps your place in it for 30 seconds, so you can come back. Because a direct call goes straight between devices, the people in it can see each other’s IP address, as with most calling apps; this can give a rough idea of where someone is, so only call people you trust. When Budgie’s relay is switched on, calls go through Cloudflare (below) instead, and the people in a call don’t see each other’s IP addresses. While a call goes through the relay, your device tells Budgie’s server every 15 seconds how much data it has received through it (just a number), which is added to the month’s total for the whole of Budgie. If that total reaches the relay’s free allowance, calls end and can’t start again until the next month; everything else keeps working.

  • Tailscale (Tailscale Inc., Canada) connects the server to the internet. Your connection is encrypted all the way to Budgie’s server, so Tailscale can’t read what you send, but it handles your IP address to route the connection. See the Tailscale Privacy Policy.
  • GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc., USA) hosts the website itself. Like any web host, GitHub receives your IP address and basic browser information when it sends you the site’s files, and may keep logs for security. See the GitHub General Privacy Statement.
  • Google (Google Ireland Limited if you’re in the EU, the UK or Switzerland, Google LLC elsewhere) provides the ads, through Google AdSense. When a feed shows an ad, your browser loads it from Google, and Google receives your IP address, details about your browser and device, and the address of the page. We never give Google your posts, messages, username or anything else from your account. We ask Google for non-personalised ads, so the ads you see aren’t based on what you’ve done before, on Budgie or elsewhere. Google still uses cookies or similar storage for them, to limit how often you see the same ad, to stop fraud and to count views and clicks. Google decides how it uses that data, as an independent controller: see How Google uses information from sites that use its services and the Google Privacy Policy.
  • Cloudflare (Cloudflare, Inc., USA) may check you’re not a robot when you create an account, with Cloudflare Turnstile, to keep out fake accounts and spam. It’s only loaded on the sign-up form, and only while it’s switched on. While that form is open, your browser talks to Cloudflare directly: Cloudflare receives your IP address and technical details about your browser and its connection (such as its user agent), and may keep a little data in your browser for the check. Budgie’s server then sends Cloudflare the one-time answer from the check, and nothing else: not your username or anything from your account. For the check itself, Cloudflare works on our behalf. It also uses these details to improve its bot detection, as an independent controller, and says it doesn’t use them to identify, profile or target anyone. See the Turnstile Privacy Addendum and the Cloudflare Privacy Policy.
  • KLIPY (Kikliko, Inc.) provides the GIF search in chats and posts, once the person who runs Budgie switches it on. When you open it, your browser asks KLIPY for GIFs directly, as KLIPY requires: KLIPY receives what you search for, your IP address, your browser’s details and your country (from your browser’s language setting), but not your username or anything else from your account. GIFs found this way, in your chats and in posts anyone sees, are loaded from KLIPY’s servers, so KLIPY also receives the IP address of whoever sees them. A post or message with such a GIF keeps only KLIPY’s link to it. Budgie always asks for KLIPY’s strictest content filter. KLIPY decides how it uses this data, as an independent controller: see the KLIPY Privacy Policy.
  • Cloudflare also helps devices find each other for calls. When you’re in a call, your device asks Cloudflare’s STUN server what its public internet address is, so Cloudflare receives your IP address, and nothing else. If the person who runs Budgie switches on its relay (Cloudflare Realtime TURN), the call’s encrypted sound and pictures pass through Cloudflare’s servers on their way, which Cloudflare can’t decode; it receives the IP addresses of the devices in the call and how much data passes. Budgie’s server may also ask Cloudflare how much data the relay has carried this month, as one total. For this, Cloudflare works on our behalf. See the Cloudflare Privacy Policy.

All of them may process data outside the EU, under their own safeguards for international transfers. Videos are converted on Budgie’s own computer with FFmpeg; they aren’t sent anywhere else. Budgie’s own fonts, icons, colour library and translations come from the same site; the only things loaded from another company are Google’s ads, Cloudflare Turnstile on the sign-up form, and GIFs from KLIPY. During a call, your device also talks to Cloudflare to connect it.

5. How long we keep it

  • Your account and what you post stay until you delete them.
  • When you delete a post or comment, its content is wiped from the server at once, with its picture or video. When you change or remove your profile picture or banner, the old one is deleted from the server at once. When you delete your account, your profile, votes, Sparks, friends, the list of who you blocked, messages (on both sides), what you said in group chats, notifications and images are deleted, you leave your groups, and your posts and comments are wiped and marked “deleted”.
  • When you leave a group chat, what you said in it stays for the others, until you delete your account. A group is deleted, with everything said in it and every picture sent in it, when its last person leaves. A picture sent in a chat is deleted with its message.
  • A ban from a nest ends when its time is up, or when the nest’s team lifts it.
  • Backups are overwritten within 14 days, so deleted things are gone from backups after that.
  • Sign-ins you don’t use for 90 days expire. Your inbox keeps your newest 200 notifications.
  • Reports and moderation decisions are kept as a record for up to a year after they’re resolved.

6. In your browser

Budgie itself doesn’t use cookies. It keeps your sign-in key in your browser’s storage, so you stay signed in, and a few small choices that make the app work the way you set it: the language you picked, the emoji you used last, whether your camera starts on in calls, and which corner your own picture sits in during a call. None of these are sent anywhere or used to follow you. They’re strictly necessary for the service you asked for, so under EU rules (the ePrivacy Directive) they don’t need consent. Signing out removes your sign-in key.

The ads are different: Google may keep cookies or similar data in your browser for them. In the EU, the UK and Switzerland, Google’s consent message asks you first. If you say no, you get limited ads that don’t use that data for advertising. You can change your answer any time in Settings → Privacy and legal → Ad cookie choices. On the sign-up form, Cloudflare Turnstile may also keep a little data in your browser, only for the robot check (section 4). Wherever you are, you can also manage Google’s ads in My Ad Center and block cookies in your browser’s settings.

7. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to access, correct, delete, restrict and move your personal data, and to object to how it’s used. You can do most of this yourself:

  • See and take a copy: Settings → Account → Export my data.
  • Correct: edit your profile, posts and comments.
  • Delete: Settings → Account → Delete account, or delete single posts, comments and photos.

For anything else, contact us (section 1). We’ll answer within a month. You also have the right to complain to a data protection authority, for example in the country where you live.

If you’re in the United States: we don’t sell your personal information, and because we ask Google for non-personalised ads, we don’t use it for targeted advertising. That’s true under the California Consumer Privacy Act and other state privacy laws.

8. Automatic checks and decisions

Budgie checks what’s posted or sent automatically, to keep it safe (see the Terms of Use, section 7): with word lists on your device and on the server, and on the server with two small AI models, one that reads text and one that looks at pictures. They run on Budgie’s own computer, and nothing is sent to another company. They only look at what’s being posted or sent at that moment, keep no record of it beyond a short reason when they report something to a moderator, and aren’t used to build a profile of you or for ads. Videos are checked by the picture model, a few frames at a time. Usernames are checked by the text model when you pick one: one it’s sure is hurtful is refused, and a doubtful one is shown to a moderator. Pictures sent in chats are checked by the picture model as strictly as profile pictures; GIFs from KLIPY, in chats or posts, aren’t checked by Budgie (KLIPY’s own content filter is set to its strictest), but can be reported. Calls aren’t checked or listened to: they never reach Budgie’s server.

They decide automatically whether something can be posted, but they don’t make decisions with legal or similarly significant effects on you: you can put a blocked post another way, and only a person can take down something already posted or ban an account. Reports are reviewed by a person. If you think a check or a decision got it wrong, contact us and a person will look at it.

9. Security

Connections to Budgie are encrypted (HTTPS), and so are calls, between the devices in them. Passwords are stored with scrypt, a slow one-way hash, and sign-in keys and recovery codes only as hashes. The server only answers Budgie’s own website and only listens behind its encrypted tunnel. If something ever goes wrong with your data, we’ll tell you and, where the law requires it, the authorities.

10. People named in the credits

Budgie’s credits name the people who made and tested it, with their agreement. If you’re named and want your name changed or removed, contact us and we’ll do it.

11. Children

Budgie isn’t meant for children under 13, and we don’t knowingly let them sign up. Between 13 and your country’s age of digital consent (up to 16 in the EU), you need a parent’s or guardian’s permission to use Budgie. Ads on Budgie aren’t personalised for anyone, whatever their age. If you believe a child under 13 has an account, contact us and we’ll delete it.

If we change this policy in a way that matters, we’ll tell you in the app before it applies. The date at the top always shows the latest version.
