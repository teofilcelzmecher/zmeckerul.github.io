Privacy Policy

Version 1.7.0 · Last updated 13 November 2026

The short version

  • Your account, posts, comments, messages and settings are kept on Budgie’s own server: a computer run by Budgie’s developer, not a big tech company.
  • We don’t ask for your email, real name, phone number or location. Budgie’s server doesn’t keep IP addresses: to enforce bans, it keeps only a scrambled form of the networks your account uses Budgie from, which can’t be turned back into the address, for 90 days (section 2).
  • Your achievements, your visit streak and the profile rewards you wear are public on your profile. What you’re listening to or playing is only shared if you use Activity: if you link Spotify or Steam, set it yourself, or use Budgie for your computer (which shares it unless you turn that off) or Budgie for iPhone (which shares what the Music app plays only if you turn that on), and only with friends unless you choose otherwise. To find it, Budgie’s server asks Spotify, Steam or Apple’s iTunes search, never your browser (section 4).
  • Budgie has no ads, on the website or in any of its apps, and nothing on it tracks you.
  • In Budgie’s apps, if you turn notifications on, notifications about messages, calls and replies reach your device through Google’s Firebase Cloud Messaging (Android) or Apple’s Push Notification service (iPhone, iPad and Mac): Google or Apple gets your device’s notification token and what each notification says, such as who a message is from and its first words (section 4). You can turn them off in your device’s settings.
  • When you create an account, Cloudflare Turnstile may check you’re not a robot, usually without you doing anything (section 4). It’s only loaded on the sign-up form.
  • Voice and video calls, and screens shared in them, are encrypted between the devices in the call and never recorded: Budgie’s server never gets the sound or pictures. When Budgie’s relay is on, calls pass through Cloudflare, so the people in a call don’t see each other’s IP address; on a direct call, they can. If the relay’s free allowance for the month runs out, calls pause until the next month (section 4).
  • Short videos you post are converted on Budgie’s own server, which drops hidden details such as where they were filmed, and checked automatically like pictures. Pictures you send in chats are kept on Budgie’s server with the message, and checked automatically. If you search for GIFs, in a chat or a post, your search goes to KLIPY, a GIF service, along with your IP address, and GIFs found that way are shown from KLIPY’s servers (section 4).
  • Budgie itself uses no cookies, analytics or tracking, and we never sell your information or give Google your posts, messages or account.
  • To keep Budgie safe, what you post and send, private messages and usernames included, is checked automatically on Budgie’s own server, by word lists and two small AI models. Nothing is sent to another company for this, and a moderator only sees a private message that’s reported or flagged (section 8).
  • You can download everything we hold about you, or delete your account, any time in Settings → Account.

4. Where it’s kept, and who else is involved

Budgie’s server is a single computer looked after by Budgie’s developer. The database on it is protected by the computer’s security, and nightly backups are kept on the same computer, with occasional copies on a storage drive in the developer’s care. As the person who runs the server, the developer can technically open the database, private messages included. We only do so to fix problems, handle reports about a specific message (including one the automatic checks flagged), or when the law requires it. The automatic checks (section 8) run on that same computer. The text model they use was downloaded once from Hugging Face, a website for sharing AI models; nothing about you or what you post is ever sent there.

Calls. To connect a call, your device and the others in it swap short technical messages through Budgie’s server: offers and answers saying which kinds of sound and video each can handle, and the network addresses each could be reached at, which include your IP address. The server passes them straight on and keeps them only in its memory, for two minutes at most. The call itself then goes directly between the devices, encrypted with WebRTC’s standard encryption, so Budgie’s server never receives its sound or pictures, or a screen someone shares. When you share your screen, the others in the call first get a small, blurred picture of it every few seconds, with a button to watch it; only those who choose to watch get it in full, with its sound if you shared that too. Reactions and soundboard sounds you send in a call go to the others as short messages through Budgie’s server, the same way, and aren’t kept. If your page closes or reloads during a call, the server keeps your place in it for 30 seconds, so you can come back. Because a direct call goes straight between devices, the people in it can see each other’s IP address, as with most calling apps; this can give a rough idea of where someone is, so only call people you trust. When Budgie’s relay is switched on, calls go through Cloudflare (below) instead, and the people in a call don’t see each other’s IP addresses. While a call goes through the relay, your device tells Budgie’s server every 15 seconds how much data it has received through it (just a number), which is added to the month’s total for the whole of Budgie. If that total reaches the relay’s free allowance, calls end and can’t start again until the next month; everything else keeps working.

5. How long we keep it

  • Your account and what you post stay until you delete them.
  • When you delete a post or comment, its content is wiped from the server at once, with its picture or video. When you change or remove your profile picture or banner, the old one is deleted from the server at once. When you delete your account, your profile, votes, Sparks, friends, the list of who you blocked, messages (on both sides), what you said in group chats, notifications and images are deleted, you leave your groups, and your posts and comments are wiped and marked “deleted”.
  • When you leave a group chat, what you said in it stays for the others, until you delete your account. A group is deleted, with everything said in it and every picture sent in it, when its last person leaves. A picture sent in a chat is deleted with its message.
  • A ban from a nest ends when its time is up, or when the nest’s team lifts it.
  • Backups are overwritten within 14 days, so deleted things are gone from backups after that.
  • Sign-ins you don’t use for 90 days expire. Your inbox keeps your newest 200 notifications.
  • A phone’s notification token is deleted when you sign out on it, when that sign-in expires, when you delete your account, or when Google or Apple says the app is gone from the device. Budgie keeps at most your ten newest devices.
  • Reports and moderation decisions are kept as a record for up to a year after they’re resolved.
  • What you’re listening to or playing disappears 3 minutes after Budgie last saw it, or when a status you typed runs out (24 hours at most). Your links to Spotify and Steam, and your Activity counts, stay until you unlink them or delete your account.
  • Your streak, achievements and rewards stay until you delete your account. The days of your streak are kept for four weeks. While a call goes on, and for 30 minutes after it ends, the server remembers in its memory when each person joined and left, only to cap the screen-sharing time it counts.
  • A sticker or emoji you made stays until you delete it (it then disappears from everyone’s library, and old messages show its name instead) or your account is deleted.
  • What you said in a nest’s channels stays until you, the nest’s team or a moderator deletes it, the channel is deleted, or your account is deleted; a picture sent there goes with its message.
  • Nudges are kept for a day. The scrambled networks your account used are deleted after 90 days, and with your account; a network ban is kept until it ends or is lifted.

6. In your browser

Budgie itself doesn’t use cookies. It keeps your sign-in key in your browser’s storage, so you stay signed in, and a few small choices that make the app work the way you set it: the language you picked, the emoji you used last, whether your camera starts on in calls, which corner your own picture sits in during a call, the volume you set for each person in calls, and the microphone, speaker, noise setting and push to talk you picked, and your sticker library and the stickers you used last, so they open quickly, and, right after you join, that you haven’t seen Budgie’s welcome yet, and, in Budgie for iPhone, whether to share what the Music app plays. It also keeps the look it last opened in (light or dark, your colours, and whether to keep things still), so it opens in your look from its very first moment, and the address of Budgie’s server, so it can ask it what’s new the moment it opens. None of these are sent anywhere or used to follow you. They’re strictly necessary for the service you asked for, so under EU rules (the ePrivacy Directive) they don’t need consent. Signing out removes your sign-in key and the look it opened in.

Budgie’s app for Windows, Mac and Linux keeps the same in its own storage on your computer, and a few things of its own: its window’s size and place, your language, whether it shows notifications and starts with your computer, and whether it shares what you’re listening to and playing (you can change these from its icon in the tray). It starts with your computer unless you turn that off, and keeps running in the tray when you close its window, so calls can ring; Quit Budgie stops it.

On the sign-up form, Cloudflare Turnstile may keep a little data in your browser, only for the robot check (section 4). You can block cookies and site data in your browser’s settings.

7. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to access, correct, delete, restrict and move your personal data, and to object to how it’s used. You can do most of this yourself:

  • See and take a copy: Settings → Account → Export my data.
  • Correct: edit your profile, posts and comments.
  • Delete: Settings → Account → Delete account, or delete single posts, comments and photos.

For anything else, contact us (section 1). We’ll answer within a month. You also have the right to complain to a data protection authority, for example in the country where you live.

If you’re in the United States: we don’t sell your personal information, and we don’t use it for targeted advertising: Budgie has no ads. That’s true under the California Consumer Privacy Act and other state privacy laws.

8. Automatic checks and decisions

Budgie checks what’s posted or sent automatically, to keep it safe (see the Terms of Use, section 7): with word lists on your device and on the server, and on the server with two small AI models, one that reads text and one that looks at pictures. They run on Budgie’s own computer, and nothing is sent to another company. They only look at what’s being posted or sent at that moment, keep no record of it beyond a short reason when they report something to a moderator, and aren’t used to build a profile of you or for ads. Videos are checked by the picture model, a few frames at a time. Usernames are checked by the text model when you pick one: one it’s sure is hurtful is refused, and a doubtful one is shown to a moderator. Pictures sent in chats, and the pictures of stickers and emojis, are checked by the picture model as strictly as profile pictures; the names of stickers and emojis, and statuses you type for Activity, are checked like a bio; GIFs from KLIPY, in chats or posts, aren’t checked by Budgie (KLIPY’s own content filter is set to its strictest), but can be reported. Calls aren’t checked or listened to: they never reach Budgie’s server.

They decide automatically whether something can be posted, but they don’t make decisions with legal or similarly significant effects on you: you can put a blocked post another way, and only a person can take down something already posted or ban an account. Reports are reviewed by a person. If you think a check or a decision got it wrong, contact us and a person will look at it.

9. Security

Connections to Budgie are encrypted (HTTPS), and so are calls, between the devices in them. Passwords are stored with scrypt, a slow one-way hash (at the strength OWASP recommends), and sign-in keys and recovery codes only as hashes. A new password can’t be one of the most used ones: it’s checked against a list kept on Budgie’s server, and never sent anywhere else. You can turn on two-step sign-in, and sign out of every other device, in Settings → Account. The server only answers Budgie’s own website and only listens behind its encrypted tunnel. If something ever goes wrong with your data, we’ll tell you and, where the law requires it, the authorities.
